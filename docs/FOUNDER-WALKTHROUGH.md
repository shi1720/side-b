# Founder walkthrough

**Presenter:** Shivam Gupta  
**Target duration:** approximately 3 minutes at a natural, steady pace  
**Preparation:** open a fresh sample workspace, set pickup instructions in shop settings, use the locally verified film anchor Past Lives, and rehearse the exact live path. Use the on-screen results actually returned. Do not read numerical outcomes that have not appeared. The spoken copy below avoids fixed result counts and financial amounts.

## Verbatim script

Hi, I'm Shivam Gupta, and this is Side B: a merchandising agent for independent record shops.

Imagine someone walks into your shop and says, “I love this film. What should I listen to next?” A great shopkeeper can make that connection. A useful collection also needs the right stock, price, and margin.

Side B brings those decisions together.

I'm starting in a private sample shop. These are real artists, with illustrative stock and prices. Shops can import up to fifty titles. Costs stay private.

Here in the studio, I search Qloo for the film Past Lives. It can be a film, book, TV show, or artist. I select the exact match.

Now I set the shop's rules: collection size, maximum price per record, minimum gross margin, and minimum cultural rank fit. I can also give older stock a second chance.

When I build the edit, the agent verifies the anchors, resolves the artists in my inventory, and asks Qloo for affinities restricted to those stocked artists. It then checks every commercial constraint and allows only one record per artist.

The result uses what this shop can offer today.

Here is the edit. “Why this record” shows Qloo evidence, the artist's candidate-pool position, and the fetch time. Cultural rank fit is a relative merchandising score. It is not a purchase probability, and artist affinity does not prove a specific album will suit someone.

I can also inspect the agent's decision trail and every excluded record. If too few titles meet my rules, Side B says so. It does not quietly relax the brief.

The financial view shows what happens if one copy of each selected record sells. It is a gross profit scenario, excluding fees, tax, and overhead. Beside it, the stock-age baseline shows how the answer changes when cultural evidence enters the decision.

I can edit the collection's title and story, add pickup instructions, then choose to publish. This creates a customer page with live availability. A customer can reserve one record for pickup. Holds last forty-eight hours. No payment happens here.

Back in the shop workspace, the hold appears. I can mark it collected, or cancel it and restore the stock. That closes the loop from cultural discovery to a real counter workflow.

The business hypothesis is a twenty-nine to forty-nine dollar monthly tool for small record shops. The next step is a retailer pilot measuring time saved and collected holds. I am not claiming traction or sales uplift before that test.

Qloo supplies the cultural connection. Side B makes that connection usable inside the shop's real constraints.

I'm Shivam Gupta. Thanks for taking Side B for a spin.
